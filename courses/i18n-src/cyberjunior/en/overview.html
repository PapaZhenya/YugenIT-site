About the course

The course prepares someone with no prior background for a confident start in cybersecurity. By the end, the student understands how networks and systems work, how they are attacked, how they are defended, and how incidents are investigated — and is ready to apply for a junior SOC analyst position or continue on the pentesting path.

Format: blended (theory + practice), 12 weeks, 6–10 hours of workload per week.

Who it's for

  • Beginners with no IT experience who need structure rather than a chaos of scattered videos.
  • Specialists from adjacent fields (sysadmins, developers) looking to move into infosec.
  • Students preparing for their first certification (CompTIA Security+ and beyond).

Design principle — the "spiral"

Each module builds on the previous one and adds complexity: first the foundation (how everything works), then attack (how it's broken), defense (how it's hardened), response (how it's investigated), and specialization (where to go next).

Workstation requirements

  • Computer: 8+ GB RAM (16 GB recommended), 60+ GB free disk space, support for virtualization (VT-x/AMD-V).
  • Software: VirtualBox or VMware Workstation Player (free).
  • Virtual machines: Kali Linux (attack) plus Metasploitable 2 and/or vulnerable web applications (target).
  • Accounts on learning platforms (see 03-resources.md).

Legal and ethical framework

⚠️ Legal and ethical framework. All attack techniques are studied EXCLUSIVELY in an isolated lab or on platforms specifically designed for this purpose. Scanning, exploiting, or testing someone else's systems without the owner's written permission is a criminal offense. This principle is repeated in every "attack" module and is part of the grading criteria.
