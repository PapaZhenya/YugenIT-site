This module of the "Specialization" block helps you get oriented among cybersecurity directions, choose a career track, and build a resume with a portfolio to break into the profession.

Module Objectives

  • Understand the different directions within infosec
  • Plan an individual development path
  • Build a resume and portfolio for a target role

Summary

Cybersecurity is a fan of directions. The task is to pick a realistic entry point and build a plan.

Main Tracks

  • SOC analyst — monitoring and response; the best entry point into the profession.
  • Pentester — offensive security, finding vulnerabilities on assignment.
  • AppSec — application and code security.
  • GRC — governance, risk, and compliance (not an offensive area).
  • Forensics / IR — incident investigation.

Certifications and portfolio. A typical trajectory: CompTIA Security+ (foundation) → CySA+ for SOC, CEH as theory or hands-on OSCP for pentesting. Early on, a portfolio is often more important than a certificate: lab and CTF writeups, a home lab, GitHub.

Deeper Dive

Main Tracks in Detail

SOC/Blue Team — monitoring and response, the best entry point into the profession. Pentest/Red Team — offensive security on assignment. AppSec — application and code security. GRC — governance, risk, and compliance (not an offensive area). Forensics/IR — incident investigation. Choose based on your disposition: like breaking things → pentest; like catching and investigating → Blue Team.

Certifications as a Trajectory

CompTIA Security+ — the foundation, widely recognized. Further along the track: CySA+ for SOC, CEH as theory or hands-on OSCP for pentesting. A certificate confirms knowledge, but early on a portfolio is often more important.

The Portfolio Matters More Than You'd Think

Writeups of solved labs and CTFs, a description of your home lab, a GitHub repository, notes — all of this demonstrates real skills and thinking better than a line on a resume. The employer sees HOW you think. Start building your portfolio during the course.

One More Example: Two Candidates for a Junior SOC Role

Neither has experience. The first completed the course and stopped there. The second completed the course, solved 30 labs with writeups, built a home lab, and posted it on GitHub. In the interview, the second candidate shows concrete work and thinking — they get hired. The takeaway: practice and portfolio decide it. A certificate helps you pass the filter, but you get hired for your skills.

Step-by-Step Walkthrough: A 6-Month Plan to Enter the Profession

  1. Finish the fundamentals (this course) and choose a track.
  2. Start preparing for Security+ (theory + practice).
  3. In parallel, solve labs (TryHackMe/HTB) and write writeups.
  4. Build a home lab and document it.
  5. Put together a resume and GitHub tailored to the target role.
  6. Apply to junior positions (SOC L1) and internships.

Mini-Project: Personal Roadmap

Put together your plan on a single page:

  • the chosen track and why;
  • 3 skills you need to build up (based on job postings);
  • a certification plan with timelines;
  • a list of 5 labs/projects for your portfolio;
  • a draft resume for a junior role.

Common Mistakes and Myths

  • Myth: you won't get hired without a certificate. A portfolio is often more important at entry level.
  • Mistake: spreading yourself across all tracks at once. Pick one and go deep.
  • Myth: you have to start as a pentester right away. Most people enter through SOC/Blue Team.
  • Mistake: studying only theory. Practice and writeups are what decide it.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Which track is easiest to start with? SOC analyst (Blue Team).
  • Which certification should I start with? CompTIA Security+.
  • Do I need a degree? Not required; skills and a portfolio carry more weight.
  • How do I get experience without a job? Labs, CTFs, a home lab, open source.
