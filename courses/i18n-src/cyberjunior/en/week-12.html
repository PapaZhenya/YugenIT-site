Summary

The capstone simulates a real-world task and serves as the flagship piece of your portfolio. It tests your ability to combine skills into coherent work.

Two Formats to Choose From

  • CTF competition — a series of challenges on a platform (picoCTF, HackTheBox), tests the breadth of your skills.
  • Full audit of a vulnerable machine — VulnHub/HackTheBox: reconnaissance → exploitation → risk assessment → recommendations → report.

The report is the key artifact. It's the report that shows the level of your thinking, not the mere fact of the breach. It contains: objective and scope, methodology, findings with evidence, a risk assessment for each finding, recommendations, and a conclusion. The order in an audit is strict: reconnaissance → exploitation → recommendations → write-up.

Deeper Dive

Why the Report Matters More Than the Breach

An employer doesn't evaluate the mere fact that you "broke into the machine" — they evaluate how you thought: how you acted, assessed risk, and formulated recommendations. The report is proof of your thinking. A poor report devalues good work; a good one makes you look like a professional even on a simple machine.

Assessing the Risk of Findings

Each finding is rated by severity (low/medium/high/critical) — based on likelihood of exploitation and impact. This lets the client fix the important things first. The ability to prioritize is a sign of a mature specialist, not someone who "found everything and left it at that".

Two Capstone Formats

CTF (picoCTF, HTB) — tests the breadth of your practical skills through challenges. Vulnerable machine audit (VulnHub/HTB) — the full cycle from reconnaissance to report. Choose based on your goal: CTF is faster and more varied; an audit is closer to real work and better for a portfolio.

One More Example: One Machine — Two Reports

Two students broke into the same machine. The first submitted a list of commands with no structure. The second submitted a report: objective, methodology, findings with evidence, risk, recommendations, conclusion. Technically the result is identical, but the second one looks like the work of a professional, while the first looks like notes. In an interview, the difference is obvious immediately. The report is your face.

Step-by-Step Walkthrough: Auditing a Machine (in the lab)

  1. Choose a vulnerable machine (VulnHub/HTB) and define the scope.
  2. Reconnaissance: scanning, mapping services and versions.
  3. Search for and exploit vulnerabilities (in the lab), documenting every step.
  4. Assess the risk of each finding.
  5. Formulate remediation recommendations.
  6. Put together a professional report and prepare a defense.
⚠️ Only use purpose-built vulnerable machines/platforms.

Mini-Project: This Is the Capstone Project Itself

The capstone project is the culmination of the course. Complete it in full:

  • choose a format (CTF or machine audit);
  • go through the entire cycle with documentation;
  • write up the report using the template from the assessment section;
  • prepare a 5-minute oral defense of your key decisions;
  • add the work to your portfolio.

Key Terms

  • Audit / pentest — a security assessment with a report.
  • CTF — a competition of practical challenges.
  • Scope — what's included in the testing.
  • Risk assessment — prioritizing findings by severity.
  • Report — the main artifact (structure above).

Common Mistakes and Myths

  • Myth: the main thing is breaking into the machine. The main thing is a well-written report and clear thinking.
  • Mistake: not prioritizing findings. The client needs to know what to fix first.
  • Myth: recommendations aren't needed, "it's obvious anyway". Recommendations are part of the work's value.
  • Mistake: testing outside the approved scope. Only use designated machines.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Should I choose CTF or an audit? CTF — for breadth of skills; audit — closer to real work and to a portfolio.
  • What if I didn't fully break into the machine? Document what you did and your conclusions — that's valuable too.
  • How important is the report? Critical: often more important than the breach itself.
  • Where do I grow from here? Specialize along the track from week 11.

Lab Work

⚠️ Legal framework. All actions must take place only in an isolated lab or on explicitly authorized platforms. Attacking systems that aren't yours without permission is illegal.

Part A. Planning

  1. Choose a format (CTF or audit) and a target.
  2. Define the scope and rules (testing boundaries).

Part B. Execution

  1. Go through the full cycle, documenting every step.
  2. Rate the vulnerabilities by severity.
  3. Formulate remediation recommendations.

Part C. Report and Defense

  1. Write up a professional report using the template.
  2. Prepare a brief defense of your key decisions.
  3. Conduct a self-reflection.

Additional Labs

Part D. CTF Practice

  1. Solve 2–3 challenges on picoCTF or HTB Starting Point.
  2. Briefly note your approach to each one.

Part E. Report Draft

  1. For one solved challenge, put together a mini-report using the full structure.
  2. Check that it includes evidence and recommendations.
