About the course "Networking from zero: how communication works"

Most people use networks every day without thinking about what happens "under the hood." This course strips away the magic: you'll understand how your message finds the right computer on the other side of the planet, and learn to build and fix networks yourself.

We deliberately don't follow the textbook order. First comes the big picture and hands-on practice, then theory and models. That way knowledge attaches to examples you already understand, instead of floating in the air.

Format: practice-oriented. 3 parts, 9 modules, 3-5 hours per module.

Who it's for

  • Anyone who wants to finally understand how the internet and home networks work.
  • Future support specialists, sysadmins, and security engineers — this is their alphabet.
  • Anyone preparing for their first networking certification.

What you'll learn

  • Explain what happens from pressing Enter to a page loading.
  • Understand addresses, names, and ports — how devices find each other.
  • Understand what a switch, a router, and an access point each do.
  • Build a working network in a simulator and find the faults in it.
  • Recognize basic threats and close off weak points.

What you'll need

  • A computer with internet access.
  • A free network simulator (for example, Cisco Packet Tracer) — to build networks "by hand."
  • A free traffic analyzer (Wireshark) — to peek at real packets.
  • Built-in system commands: ping, tracert/traceroute, ipconfig/ip, nslookup.
Practice only on your own devices and in the simulator. Interfering with someone else's network without permission is not allowed: it's illegal.
